# StreetSkate / Chimp Hawk Underground — graphics and art direction audit

Date: 11 October 2026. Historical source inspected: `17259ec` in `G:/Streetskate/warehouse-update`. This local revision is not the authoritative production baseline.

**User-confirmed baseline and scope:** the current live production version of `main` is the best version so far and the baseline for enhancements. Scope is exactly its three maps: Skyline Rooftop, ORBIT and Grand Warehouse. Preserve the current successful visuals and gameplay. Prefer sustained 144 FPS for 144 Hz displays, with an approximately 6.94 ms frame budget on a documented target desktop. Recheck every historical finding below against production before using it to justify changes; older screenshots and local branches must not override the live baseline.

## Verdict

The game has a distinctive identity: expressive stylized riders, graffiti lettering, a sunset rooftop, an orbital terminal, and an industrial skatepark. The next quality leap should come from consistent art direction, readable silhouettes, believable material response, and deliberate composition. Adding texture resolution, bloom, reflections, and props everywhere would amplify the current visual competition.

Keep the playful character proportions and skate culture. Aim for a polished stylized arcade game with tactile surfaces and cinematic lighting that remains readable at skating speed. Use the title art as a palette and mood reference, while matching promotional character detail to what the game can actually deliver.

## Proposed visual system

**Composition:** rider and next usable obstacle first; gameplay feedback second; atmosphere third. Dense detail belongs at rest points and boundaries. Traversal corridors need quiet surfaces and clean silhouettes.

**Palette:** a shared ink base (`#101421`), warm neutral text (`#F6EBD9`), cyan (`#63DCE8`), violet (`#A36BE7`), and gold (`#F4C44F`). These are starting tokens, not calibrated final colors. Reserve gold for primary actions, cyan for focus, and a check/label for selection. Warn with warm orange; communicate bail with shape/text as well as red. Each map keeps its own lighting identity.

**Typography:** one readable body family, one condensed display family, and the existing decorative graffiti assets. Functional UI should use real text. Body text starts around 14–16 CSS px, secondary labels around 12–13 px; test actual contrast and viewport constraints before locking values. Aim for 4.5:1 text contrast and 3:1 for large text/control boundaries. Preserve keyboard/controller focus independently of selection.

**Material language:** three spatial scales: macro wear and color variation; medium panel joints and skate paths; subtle micro roughness. Do not bake strong directional shadows into tileable albedo. Start with 1K–2K shared surface sets and 512–1K prop atlases; reserve higher resolution for demonstrated closeup need. These are budget proposals, not current measurements.

**Motion:** short restrained menu transitions, readable score banking and landing cues; no constant camera shake or decorative motion behind critical text. Reduced-motion preference also needs a reduced-cost static menu backdrop, since `LoadoutBackdrop` currently keeps its 24 Hz loop even when pointer motion is suppressed.

New props need a clear visual/collision role and source/license record. Decorative meshes must not accidentally become skateable through name-based import filters. Use instancing/atlases when compatible with culling and material needs; measure rather than assuming merging always helps.

## Practical sequence

1. **Readability pass:** reliable functional text; score/HUD hierarchy; smaller music toast; calibrated rider lighting; honest effective graphics settings. Capture before/after at unchanged camera and resolution.
2. **One vertical slice:** perfect one rooftop ramp/rail route, one ORBIT bank/coping section, and one warehouse timber/stair bay. Compare silhouette, lighting, UV scale, wear and contact before propagating the style.
3. **Material and geometry pass:** separate material signals, author bounded decal variation, inspect bevels/normals, and protect existing collision. Keep a rollback path for each asset change.
4. **Composition and props:** add coherent groups and landmarks only after route review; produce genuine map thumbnails and matching character/deck previews.
5. **Measured render polish:** evaluate local shadow coverage, selective bloom, optional reflections and compression on representative devices. Choose improvements by visible benefit per frame cost.

## Acceptance plan

Capture title, loadout, options, pause, tutorial, results, and active skating at 1920×1080, 1366×768, 1280×720, 844×390 and 390×844. Narrow UI testing is not proof of touch gameplay support. Test keyboard and controller focus, 200% text zoom, failed glyph/portrait requests and reduced motion.

For each selectable map: capture spawn, ramp approach, rail approach, bowl/vert transition and a prop closeup; include all default selectable riders and representative neutral/saturated deck finishes. Compare at the same graphics tier, camera, pose and exposure. Check opaque ramps, stable normals, rail silhouettes, floor texture scale and grounding with bloom both on and off.

Use a documented representative desktop with a verified 144 Hz display and a lower-powered integrated-GPU device. Preferred target, to validate rather than promise: sustained 144 FPS at 1920×1080 on the enhanced gameplay preset, approximately 6.94 ms per frame, with p95 at or below that budget on representative warmed-up routes. Record median, p95, p99, missed frames, CPU/GPU timings where supported, long stalls, draw calls, triangles, texture allocations and cold load time. Compare with the verified live production baseline and preserve existing high-refresh performance. Provide explicit supported 120/90/60 FPS fallbacks where necessary; do not make 30 FPS the design target. Preserve fixed-step gameplay timing and use interpolation for presentation where needed. Higher tiers must not silently bypass user-disabled effects. Revisit the existing intentional 1024-shadow constraint before changing it.

Deliver comparison images, asset provenance, source changes, visual findings and actual measured limitations. Physics/collision checks become necessary when geometry, scale, collision metadata or obstacle placement changes; an audit-only document does not need gameplay tests.
